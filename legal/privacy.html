1. Who We Are and Who This Covers

KinesioCal, LLC ("KinesioCal," "we") provides scheduling, booking, client-records, and practice-management software to licensed massage therapists and other service businesses. This policy explains what personal information we collect, how we use it, and the choices you have. It applies to our websites, the KinesioCal application, embedded booking portals, and our support channels.

Three kinds of people interact with KinesioCal, and your rights depend on which you are:

  • Subscribers and Users — the businesses that pay for KinesioCal and the staff they authorize. We collect your information directly and we decide how it is used; we are the "controller" for it.
  • Clients — members of the public who book or pay for a Subscriber's services. There are two kinds of Client data, with different roles. The business's copy: the business you booked with collects your booking, intake, and treatment records and decides how they are used; KinesioCal processes that copy on the business's behalf as a "processor" or "service provider," and questions about it go to the business. Your own account: if you create a KinesioCal account, you hold your own copy of your contact details, booking history, and records you have claimed, independent of any business. KinesioCal is the controller of that copy and of your account, deals with you directly about it, and gives you the notices and rights described in this policy without going through a business.
  • Visitors — anyone who browses our marketing site or contacts us without an account.

Where this policy conflicts with a signed agreement with a Subscriber (such as a Business Associate Agreement), the signed agreement controls for that Subscriber's data.

2. Our HIPAA Position

KinesioCal is built with HIPAA standards in mind. The application is designed around the HIPAA Security Rule's administrative, physical, and technical safeguards as a reference standard. We do not represent that the Service currently meets every requirement of the Security Rule, and the safeguards in place may change as the Service develops. As of the date of this policy:

Safeguards in place today

  • connections to the Service are made over HTTPS;
  • role-based access, so staff see only the records their role requires;
  • an audit log of who viewed, created, changed, or exported client records;
  • minimum-necessary design in notes, messaging, and exports;
  • separation of each Subscriber's data from every other Subscriber's;
  • automatic session timeouts.

Safeguards planned, some of which may be added during the beta period

  • password-strength requirements and multi-factor authentication for Subscriber accounts;
  • encryption of data at rest;
  • a consent and delegation record for every shared or linked client account;
  • a documented risk assessment and incident-response plan.

We will update this list as safeguards are added, and we will not offer a Business Associate Agreement until the planned safeguards are in place. [BUILD/CONSULT: confirm HTTPS is enforced on all endpoints; move items between lists only when they are live in production]

Whether HIPAA applies to a Subscriber depends on that Subscriber's own activities, chiefly whether it transmits health information electronically in connection with insurance billing. Subscribers that are HIPAA covered entities must not store protected health information in KinesioCal until a Business Associate Agreement is in place. We intend to offer a BAA on the Premium Tier once our subprocessor agreements and risk assessment are complete.

KinesioCal is not currently HIPAA-certified. Our goal is to get certified in the future but that is a process that takes time. We make no claim that using KinesioCal makes any business HIPAA-compliant. We do treat your data as health information and protect it to the best of our abilities. We do not sell Personal Indentifying Information (PII) or client data.[CONSULT: confirm this positioning; some attorneys prefer "we will act as a business associate only under a signed BAA" phrasing]

4. How We Use Information, and What We Will Never Do

We will never sell Client Personal identifying Information (PII) or Health Information. We do not sell, rent, or trade any Client's name, contact details, health information, booking history, or payment information to anyone, for any purpose. We do not share Client information with advertisers or data brokers. We do not train AI or machine-learning models on Client information, and we do not sell, license, or intentionally provide Client information to any AI company or other third party for use as training data. This commitment also applies to Subscriber and User information.

We use information only to:

  • provide the Service: schedule appointments, send confirmations and reminders, store records, process payroll, deliver safety alerts;
  • send transactional messages on a Subscriber's behalf (reminders, receipts, recapture messages the Subscriber configures);
  • bill Subscribers and prevent fraud;
  • respond to support requests, including with AI-assisted tools (see "How we use AI tools" below);
  • maintain security, investigate violations of our Terms of Service, and comply with law;
  • improve the Service using de-identified, aggregated statistics that cannot be linked to any person or business;
  • send Subscribers product updates, which you can opt out of at any time.

How we use AI tools. KinesioCal is a small company and uses third-party AI services in two ways: to help build and maintain the software, and to help triage, draft, and in some cases respond to support requests and bug reports. These are services provided by outside companies, such as but not limited to Anthropic and Open AI, under their own terms and privacy policies, which we do not control. We take the following steps to limit what those services receive:

  • We review each AI provider's published terms before using it and choose providers and settings that, by those terms, limit retention and training on inputs. As our client base grows, we intend to move to business or developer terms that provide stronger contractual data protections.
  • Support and bug-report forms instruct users not to include Client health information, and we do not need it to resolve most issues. Where Client names or contact details appear in a request and are not needed, we remove or replace them before using an AI tool.
  • Day-to-day software development uses synthetic or de-identified test data, not Client records. During large system updates, we may use AI tools to write and test the code that migrates existing account data to a new structure; in the normal case that code runs on our systems and the Client records themselves are not sent to the AI provider. If a migration requires an AI tool to process actual Client records (for example, to reformat free-text entries), we will limit it to the fields involved, exclude health information unless the provider is bound by a Business Associate Agreement, and note the migration in our release notes. [CONSULT: whether a migration that sends identifiable records to an AI provider requires advance notice to Subscribers under state privacy law or the Terms of Service]
  • We do not permit any AI service to retain Client PII beyond what its terms require to process the request.

We cannot guarantee how a third-party provider handles data on its own systems, and we do not promise that no AI system will ever process your information. We do promise that we will not sell it, will not provide it for training, and will keep the amount that reaches any AI tool to the minimum needed to help you. We share personal information only in these cases:

Service providers (subprocessors). Companies that host or support the Service under contracts that restrict them to our instructions and require appropriate security. Our current subprocessors are listed at [subprocessors page] and include: cloud hosting ([provider]), payment processing (Stripe), email and SMS delivery ([provider]), mapping and travel-time computation ([Valhalla/OpenStreetMap self-hosted; HERE or TomTom on paid tiers — only addresses, no client identity]), and error monitoring ([provider]). We will update the list before adding a subprocessor that handles Client data. [CONSULT: subprocessor list must be complete and BAAs in place for any that touch PHI]

The Subscriber you booked with. Client information is always visible to the Subscriber that collected it and to the Users that Subscriber authorizes.

Safety contacts and emergency services. For mobile and outcall appointments, the practitioner's business may designate safety contacts, who can be anyone the business chooses (a colleague, family member, or friend) and need not be employees. When the lone-worker safety feature is active, the practitioner's location, session status, and the Client's appointment address and time may be shared with those safety contacts and with the business. If the practitioner signals an emergency, those details may be shared with emergency services. A business that designates a safety contact is responsible for choosing that person and for what they do with the information.

Linked accounts. Where a Client has authorized another person (for example, a parent or caregiver) to manage their appointments or records through a linked account or family card, we share the delegated information as that consent record specifies, and no more.

Law enforcement, licensing boards, and legal process. We disclose information to government agencies only as described in Section 5 of our Terms of Service: in response to valid legal process served on KinesioCal, LLC; to prevent imminent death or serious injury; or when we report an account we have a good-faith belief is being used for trafficking, sexual services, or services involving minors. We notify affected Subscribers of requests unless prohibited or unsafe to do so.

The Safety Network. Subscribers may block a Client and share that block with other participating Subscribers under a category (for example: no-show, chargeback abuse, inappropriate conduct, safety concern, solicitation). No block information is sent to any other Subscriber until the blocked Client attempts to book with that Subscriber; at that point the receiving Subscriber sees the booking details used in the attempt and a log entry that the Client was blocked by a network share, and the Client sees a generic unavailability message. The identity of the sharing Subscriber and its notes are not disclosed to the receiving Subscriber. Clients may challenge a network share as described in the Client Terms of Use. Safety Network data, including block records, categories, and any documentation a business attaches, may be shared with law enforcement and with professional licensing or regulatory boards, either in response to legal process or when we report suspected sexual solicitation, trafficking, threats, assault, or services involving minors. Individual Safety Network records are never sold or published and are not shared with anyone else outside participating Subscribers. We may publish de-identified, aggregated statistics drawn from the Safety Network, such as the number of blocks shared by category, region, or time period, or the rate at which challenges succeed, to inform the profession and the public about practitioner safety. Published statistics will never identify a Client, a business, or a practitioner, and we will suppress any figure small enough that an individual could reasonably be inferred from it. 6. Security, Retention, and Deletion

Security. We use the safeguards described in Section 2, plus automatic session timeouts, and regular dependency and vulnerability updates. No system is perfectly secure; if we learn of a breach affecting your information, we will notify affected Subscribers within [72] hours of confirming it, with the information they need to meet their own obligations: what happened, which Clients and data fields were affected, when, and what we have done in response. For the business's copy of Client data, Subscribers are responsible for notifying their own Clients and regulators as their licensing rules and state law require; KinesioCal does not send those notices on a Subscriber's behalf. For Clients who hold a KinesioCal account, KinesioCal will notify those Clients directly, within the time state law requires, of any breach affecting their account or their own copy of their records. [CONSULT: Indiana (IC 24-4.9) and Illinois (PIPA) breach-notice timelines and content; HIPAA Breach Notification Rule if a BAA is in place]

Retention for Subscribers. We keep a Subscriber's data for as long as its account is active, with two exceptions. Archiving: records are moved to archive [18 to 24] months after their date, depending on record type (for example, an appointment is measured from the appointment date). Archived records can be retrieved on request to support, but every record may be permanently deleted 30 months after its date, regardless of when it was last viewed or retrieved. Viewing or requesting a copy of a record does not reset or extend this period, and we do not promise to keep any record beyond 30 months from its date. Closed and unpaid accounts: if a Subscriber cancels, its account is closed. If a Subscriber stops paying, it has a [3]-day grace period during which it can still use the portal and export its records; after that, portal access is limited to a page where it can reactivate its subscription at any time. In either case the business may obtain a complete export of its records, including Client records, by contacting support at [support email] within 90 days of closure or of the end of the grace period. After that, we may delete the account's data from production systems, and backups age out on their normal cycle. Reactivating an unpaid account before deletion restores access to the data that remains. We may retain records longer where required for legal holds, Safety Network records, tax and financial records, ongoing investigations, or accounts terminated for prohibited use. [BUILD: the 90-day deletion of closed and unpaid accounts and the 30-month record deletion are both stated as policy; confirm the deletion jobs exist before launch or keep "may delete" wording.]

Record-retention rules are the Subscriber's responsibility. State licensing boards require practitioners to keep client records for set periods, often several years. KinesioCal is not the custodian of those records for that purpose and does not retain them on a Subscriber's behalf beyond the export window above. Subscribers must export and keep their own copies to meet those rules.

Client accounts are independent of businesses. A Client who creates a KinesioCal account owns that account. It is not tied to any one business: a Client may book with several businesses from one account, and if a business closes or deletes its KinesioCal account, the Client's own account and personal records remain until the Client asks us to close them. A business's copy of a Client's records (intake answers, treatment notes, appointment history with that business) belongs to the business and is deleted or archived with the business's account as described above.

Client deletion requests. Because a Client's information may exist in two places, deletion takes two steps. (1) To delete the records a business holds about you, contact that business; it owns those records and may be legally required to keep some of them for a set period. (2) To close your own KinesioCal account and delete the personal information in it, use the account-closure option in your profile or email [privacy email]; we will close it within [30] days, except for Safety Network records and records we must keep by law. Closing your account removes your own copy of your records and your login; it does not remove the copy held by any business you booked with, which remains that business's record of you until the business deletes it or closes. We may also delete a Client account that was created but never used to complete a booking 90 days after creation. A Client account with no activity for 24 months has its login deactivated, after we attempt to email the account holder; the Client reverts to a guest, the records each Subscriber holds about them are unchanged, and they may create a new login at any time. Activity means any action on the account or its records by the Client or a Subscriber, including a booking, charge, sign-in, message, or record change. If you never created a KinesioCal account and booked only as a guest, you exist in the Service only as a record held by the business, and the business deleting your Client profile removes your data from the Service.
