1. Who This Covers

These Client Terms of Use ("Client Terms") apply when you book, pay for, or manage appointments with a business that uses KinesioCal ("KinesioCal," "we"), including through a booking page or widget on that business's own website, through email or text confirmations, or through a KinesioCal client account. By booking, you accept these Client Terms.

Three parties are involved:

  • You — the client booking a service.
  • The business — the licensed massage therapist or other provider you are booking with. The business is your service provider. It sets its prices, policies, and availability, holds your records, and is responsible for the service you receive.
  • KinesioCal — the software the business uses to run its schedule and records. We are not a party to your appointment, we do not provide services, and we do not employ or vouch for any business. We do not handle your money: when you pay, your payment goes through the business's own payment processor (such as Stripe) directly to the business, and KinesioCal never receives, holds, or refunds your funds. We process your booking and record information on the business's behalf.

You must be 18 or older to create a client account. A parent or guardian may book for a minor through the business's intake process.

2. Booking, Payment, and Cancellation

The business's policies govern your appointment. Deposits, cancellation windows, no-show fees, late arrival, refunds, and tipping are set by the business and shown to you before you confirm. KinesioCal does not set or enforce them and cannot issue refunds on a business's behalf; contact the business directly.

Payments. Card payments are processed by Stripe. The business is the merchant; its name appears on your statement. KinesioCal never sees your full card number and takes no portion of your payment. Disputes about a charge should go to the business first.

Accurate information. You will book under your own legal name and provide your own working email address and phone number, and you will complete any intake or health form honestly. The business relies on that information to contact you, to send confirmations and reminders, and to treat you safely. You may not book under a false or borrowed name; enter an email, phone number, or address that is not yours, including the business's own contact details, a placeholder such as example.com, or a disposable address; or create a second account to avoid a block. If you do, the business may cancel the appointment, decline service, charge any cancellation or no-show fee under its policies, and block you, and KinesioCal may close your account under Section 4. Neither the business nor KinesioCal is responsible for confirmations, reminders, receipts, or notices that fail to reach you because the contact details you gave were wrong.

ID checks. Any business may ask you to show a government ID matching your booking name when you arrive, for the practitioner's safety, whether or not it warned you in advance. Businesses can choose to display an ID-check notice at booking; a business that does not display the notice has not given up the right to ask. The business, not KinesioCal, is asking for and reviewing your ID; KinesioCal does not collect or store ID images or numbers, only whether a check was completed. Booking for others; linked accounts. A parent or guardian who books for a minor is responsible for the minor's information and conduct under these Client Terms. Who to contact. 5. The Safety Network

KinesioCal includes a Safety Network that lets businesses warn each other about clients who have been blocked. Here is how it works.

A business can block you. Any business may block you from its own booking page at any time, with or without giving a reason. That block applies only to that business and is entirely that business's decision: KinesioCal will not review, undo, or remove a block a business has placed on its own booking page, and you cannot appeal it to us. If you believe a block was a mistake, contact the business directly. Each business decides whether to use the Safety Network at all.

A business can share a block. When a business blocks you, it may also share the block with the Safety Network under a category (for example: no-show, chargeback abuse, inappropriate conduct, safety concern, solicitation). It may share with or without supporting documentation, and the network records which it is.

Other businesses choose what to accept. Each participating business sets which categories it will honor and whether it will honor blocks that have no documentation attached. A business that does not participate, or does not accept the category or documentation level of your block, is unaffected.

No information travels until you try to book. Your block is not broadcast. When you attempt to book with a participating business that accepts your block's category, that business receives your booking details and a log entry stating that you were blocked by a Safety Network share. You receive a generic message that booking is not available. The business that blocked you is not identified to the new business, and the new business does not receive the original business's documentation if any exists. 6. Challenging a Safety Network Block

You have the right to challenge the network share of any block. You cannot challenge the original business's own decision to block you; that is its choice and it stands regardless of the outcome. A successful challenge removes the share so that other businesses no longer see it.

How to challenge. Sign in to your client account and use the "Challenge network block" link at the bottom of your profile. Tell us, in your own words, what happened. You may attach messages, receipts, or other evidence.

What happens next.

  1. We notify the business that shared the block that a challenge has been filed. We do not share your statement with the business unless you ask us to.
  2. If the business shared the block without documentation, it has [14] days to provide supporting evidence of its claim. If it provides none, the share is rescinded.
  3. If the business shared the block with documentation, or provides it within the window, we review the documentation against the category claimed. If the documentation supports the claim, the share stands. If it does not, the share is rescinded.
  4. We tell you the result through the challenge tool in your account, or by the method you used to contact us if you could not use the tool. We do not give you the business's evidence, but we will tell you the category and whether documentation existed.

Repeat reports. If the same or a substantially similar report is shared by three different businesses for the same category of conduct, the network share will stand and a further challenge will not be accepted. Separate businesses independently reporting the same conduct is treated as corroboration. After a rescission. If a different business later shares a new block, you may challenge that one on its own. Each share is reviewed separately; a rescinded share is not counted toward the three-report rule. [decide: whether a rescinded share counts toward three]

Timing. We review challenges as promptly as reasonably possible. Review time depends on how many challenges are pending and on how quickly the business responds, and we do not commit to a fixed timeline. While a challenge is pending, the share remains in effect.

Final decisions. KinesioCal's decision on a network share is final within the platform. It does not affect any legal rights you or the business may have.
